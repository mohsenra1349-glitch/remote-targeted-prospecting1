01 / Overview

A combination of software and mental map dowsing

My approach brings together two parts: examining aerial or satellite imagery with specialized software, and applying my own mental process to the mapped area. I use visible terrain patterns, environmental features and my observations as part of the process of selecting a location for further investigation.

The result is a prediction marked on a map. The predicted location can then be checked independently in the field using a metal detector or other suitable equipment.

What I am — and am not — claiming

This is an unconventional, experimental form of map dowsing. I do not claim that ordinary aerial or satellite images directly detect buried gold or metal, or that this method has been scientifically established.

The central question is whether the combined approach can produce accurate, repeatable predictions under fair testing conditions.

02 / Workflow

How I approach a target area

The process is intended to turn an initial area of interest into a specific, testable map prediction.

STEP 01

Choose an area

Select an area for preliminary review and obtain suitable aerial or satellite imagery.

STEP 02

Review the imagery

Use specialized software to examine the image, visible terrain patterns and environmental features.

STEP 03

Apply the mental process

Combine the visual review with my own mental map-dowsing process to select a potential target location.

STEP 04

Mark the prediction

Mark the predicted location and define its boundaries before the field result is known.

STEP 05

Independent field check

Have another person check the marked area using appropriate equipment, without being guided toward a preferred result.

STEP 06

Record the outcome

Document the result, including misses and inconclusive tests, so the method can be evaluated honestly.

03 / Example predictions

Predictions should be recorded before testing

Examples can be presented with the original map, the marked prediction, the date it was recorded and the later independent field result.

For transparency, each example should clearly distinguish between a prediction, a detector response and a confirmed recovery. A detector signal alone does not establish that the target is gold.

04 / Independent testing

Let the results speak

A meaningful test should be designed so that neither the target location nor the expected result can influence the prediction.

  • An independent tester selects target and control locations. The locations and target details are kept hidden from me.
  • I submit a marked prediction before the target locations are revealed. The prediction is dated and preserved.
  • The tester checks the locations using agreed equipment and a pre-defined search procedure.
  • Success criteria — including target size, search area and acceptable distance from the target — are agreed in advance.
  • All outcomes are recorded and compared with an appropriate chance baseline. Unsuccessful tests are included.

Open to constructive criticism

I welcome independent testing, practical questions and scientific criticism. A negative or inconclusive result is also useful information.

The purpose is not to ask anyone to accept an extraordinary claim, but to find out whether the method performs reliably beyond chance.
